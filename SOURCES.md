## What Each Publish Value Means

- `full`: every field the pipeline takes from the source may appear in `data/` and on the site, with a source badge and provenance.
- `derived-only`: short factual values may appear with a source badge. These are names and aliases, country and sector values, motivation, dates and identifiers. The source's free text is never copied. For ETDA, that excludes `description`, `information` and the `activity` text of `operations`. This value limits what is shown. It does not reduce the licence duties: attribution, NonCommercial and ShareAlike apply exactly as they would for `full`.
- `link-only`: only an item's title, publisher, publication date and link appear, plus identifiers this project computes itself, such as a content hash. The title may be read to find the names of actors the site already publishes, and such a link is shown apart from the tagged ones. This applies to every link-only source, ORKL included.
- `evidence-only`: nothing from the source appears in `data/`. The source only adds evidence for merging actor aliases, which the site reports as a count.

## Why ETDA and Malpedia Data Is Shown

Both licences allow sharing, including of adapted material, "for NonCommercial purposes only". CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 defines NonCommercial as "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation." Section 4(c) of the 3.0 licence uses almost the same words: "primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or private monetary compensation." This site meets that definition while it stays a personal research project: no advertising, no paid access, no sponsorship, and no placement on or promotion by a business's website or services. That is a reading of the licence text, not legal advice.

Share-alike applies to the published data. The pipeline normalizes ETDA and Malpedia values, such as aliases and malware family names, and merges them with other sources in `actors/<id>.json`, `actors/index.json` and `resolution.json`. Malpedia library dates also date ORKL reports in `reports/*.json`. This project treats the result as Adapted Material, which CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 defines as material "derived from or based upon the Licensed Material and in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified". Under the 3.0 licence, the merge is not a "Collection", because a Collection includes the work "in its entirety in unmodified form".

One licence satisfies both sources. Section 3(b)(1) of the 4.0 licence requires: "The Adapter's License You apply must be a Creative Commons license with the same License Elements, this version or later, or a BY-NC-SA Compatible License." Section 4(b) of the 3.0 licence allows "a later version of this License with the same License Elements as this License". The published data in `data/` is therefore offered under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

The other sources fit inside that licence:

- MISP and KEV are CC0.
- EPSS asks only for a credit, which the notice gives, and its scores are shown unchanged.
- For adapted material, the CC BY 4.0 of the paper and of Microsoft's table (section 3(a)(4)) requires that "the Adapter's License You apply must not prevent recipients of the Adapted Material from complying with this Public License." BY-NC-SA 4.0 keeps its attribution intact.
- ATT&CK values stay under MITRE's licence, so MITRE's copyright designation and licence paragraph travel with every copy of `data/`. This covers the Enterprise, ICS and Mobile matrices, which are three files in the one `attack-stix-data` repository and share its single `LICENSE.txt`.

Share-alike does not reach the code. The pipeline and site code are not adaptations of the data, so the code licence is chosen separately. The repository must not apply a code licence to `data/`.

No extra restrictions may be added. Section 3(b)(3) of the 4.0 licence says: "You may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, Adapted Material that restrict exercise of the rights granted under the Adapter's License You apply." So `data/` carries no terms beyond CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the notices above. Anyone who reuses `data/` receives the same NonCommercial and ShareAlike terms and must credit the sources.

If the site moves to a commercial host, set `etda` and `malpedia` to `evidence-only` in this table and rebuild. Any report dated from the Malpedia library then falls back to its next date basis. Copies already published remain under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for anyone who received them, including in the git history, so a commercial deployment must be built from the rebuilt data.

## Vendor Blog Feeds

Three vendor blogs are read as `derived-only` sources: Cisco Talos (`talos`), the research posts of ESET WeLiveSecurity (`eset`) and the Microsoft Security blog (`microsoftblog`). None of them grants a licence to reuse its posts, and their terms are ambiguous about links. They were added at the maintainer's decision, on the view that a post's title, date and link are facts about a publicly released post, and that a link sends readers to the publisher. A post's title is also matched against the names of published actors. A post's text is read in memory for one thing only: a sentence that says two names are one actor, such as "JadePuffer, tracked by Microsoft as Storm-3168". The two names are kept and the sentence is dropped. Each can be removed on request or if its terms change, by deleting its entry in this table, in `pipeline/aptx/sources/vendors.py` and in the places listed there, then rebuilding.

The pipeline reads only each public RSS feed, once per build, and saves three fields per post: title, link and date, plus the two names of each pair a post states. It never saves the excerpt, categories, author or any sentence of the post, and it never reads the pages a post links to. ESET's feed covers its whole blog, so only posts under `/en/eset-research/` are kept and its scam and consumer advice posts are dropped. A post that Malpedia or ATT&CK also cites joins that report. A stated pair is used only when it names an actor the site already publishes, which adds the other name as an alias, or when it names a vendor cluster ID such as Storm-3168 beside a name no source lists and a name guesser calls an actor, which adds a new actor. A cluster ID in a title (UAT- in a Talos title, Storm- or DEV- in a Microsoft title) that no source lists also adds an actor, marked unconfirmed until a source or pair names it. A pair is never used to join two actors the sources already keep apart or to turn a malware family into an actor, and a vendor post never adds an actor tag to a report on its own. SentinelOne Labs was considered and left out: its terms bar access "by any means other than SentinelOne's publicly supported interfaces (for example, "scraping")" and say nothing that makes an automated feed read one. Unit 42, Sekoia, Elastic Security Labs and Check Point Research were not added because their terms bar republishing or linking without permission, and Securelist was not added because its robots.txt disallows `*/feed`.

## ORKL status

ORKL is `link-only` while a permission request is pending (sent 2026-09-29 to orkl@mailbox.org). Only each report's own title, publication date and link to the original publisher appear, because those are facts about publicly released reports, whose authors retain copyright. ORKL's threat-actor tags are used only as matching evidence and are not displayed. Report-to-actor links shown on the site come from sources that allow publishing them, such as Malpedia's family references and MITRE ATT&CK, and from a report title or text that names a published actor, which includes ORKL's own titles and text but never its tags. ORKL's text is read once at fetch to find those names. Only which actors were named is kept, never the text. The site also publishes CVE and ATT&CK technique IDs for ORKL reports, because the pipeline finds them itself by matching patterns in the report text, and the Explore table filters and searches on them together with those actor links. None of these fields is copied from ORKL's own record.
