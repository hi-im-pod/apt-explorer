## What Each Publish Value Means

- `full`: every field the pipeline takes from the source may appear in `data/` and on the site, with a source badge and provenance.
- `derived-only`: short factual values may appear with a source badge. These are names and aliases, country and sector values, motivation, dates and identifiers. The source's free text is never copied. For ETDA, that excludes `description`, `information` and the `activity` text of `operations`. This value limits what is shown. It does not reduce the licence duties: attribution, NonCommercial and ShareAlike apply exactly as they would for `full`.
- `link-only`: only an item's title, publisher, publication date and link appear, plus identifiers this project computes itself, such as a content hash.
- `evidence-only`: nothing from the source appears in `data/`. The source only adds evidence for merging actor aliases, which the site reports as a count.

## Why ETDA and Malpedia Data Is Shown

Both licences allow sharing, including of adapted material, "for NonCommercial purposes only". CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 defines NonCommercial as "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation." Section 4(c) of the 3.0 licence uses almost the same words: "primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or private monetary compensation." This site meets that definition while it stays a personal research project: no advertising, no paid access, no sponsorship, and no placement on or promotion by a business's website or services. That is a reading of the licence text, not legal advice.

Share-alike applies to the published data. The pipeline normalizes ETDA and Malpedia values, such as aliases and malware family names, and merges them with other sources in `actors/<id>.json`, `actors/index.json` and `resolution.json`. Malpedia library dates also date ORKL reports in `reports/*.json`. This project treats the result as Adapted Material, which CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 defines as material "derived from or based upon the Licensed Material and in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified". Under the 3.0 licence, the merge is not a "Collection", because a Collection includes the work "in its entirety in unmodified form".

One licence satisfies both sources. Section 3(b)(1) of the 4.0 licence requires: "The Adapter's License You apply must be a Creative Commons license with the same License Elements, this version or later, or a BY-NC-SA Compatible License." Section 4(b) of the 3.0 licence allows "a later version of this License with the same License Elements as this License". The published data in `data/` is therefore offered under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

The other sources fit inside that licence:

- MISP and KEV are CC0.
- For adapted material, the paper's CC BY 4.0 (section 3(a)(4)) requires that "the Adapter's License You apply must not prevent recipients of the Adapted Material from complying with this Public License." BY-NC-SA 4.0 keeps its attribution intact.
- ATT&CK values stay under MITRE's licence, so MITRE's copyright designation and licence paragraph travel with every copy of `data/`.

Share-alike does not reach the code. The pipeline and site code are not adaptations of the data, so the code licence is chosen separately. The repository must not apply a code licence to `data/`.

No extra restrictions may be added. Section 3(b)(3) of the 4.0 licence says: "You may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, Adapted Material that restrict exercise of the rights granted under the Adapter's License You apply." So `data/` carries no terms beyond CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the notices above. Anyone who reuses `data/` receives the same NonCommercial and ShareAlike terms and must credit the sources.

If the site moves to a commercial host, set `etda` and `malpedia` to `evidence-only` in this table and rebuild. Any report dated from the Malpedia library then falls back to its next date basis. Copies already published remain under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for anyone who received them, including in the git history, so a commercial deployment must be built from the rebuilt data.
